# Code of Conduct

## Our community

There isn't one. This tool was made to solve the development problems of Lanterna
Games, and was left where you could see it. A code of conduct needs people to conduct,
a committee to enforce it and somewhere to send the reports. The project is short of
all three.

## Our pledge

Nothing is pledged. In return, nothing is asked of you. It is the fairest deal in open
source.

## Our standards

Behaviour that is welcome here:

- Taking the code.
- Leaving.

Behaviour that is not:

- Asking when the next release is. There has not been a previous one.
- "+1", "any update?" and "this is a blocker for us". It is not a blocker for us.
- Announcing that the project looks abandoned. It was never adopted.
- Explaining what a real maintainer would do. A real maintainer would, yes.

## Enforcement responsibilities

The community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. The
position has been open since the first commit. It is unpaid, has no authority and
nobody to exercise it on. Apply by forking.

## Scope

This code applies in all the spaces of the community, every one of which is empty.

## Enforcement

Reports may be sent to the community leaders, who do not exist and will review them
promptly. The enforcement ladder was shortened to fit the budget:

### 1. Being ignored

**Community impact**: any.

**Consequence**: none. This is also what happens when there is no impact, which keeps
the process fair and the paperwork light.

## The one sincere line

Be decent to people, here and wherever you take this code. That part is not a joke. It
just never needed a committee.
